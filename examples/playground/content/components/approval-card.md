Human-in-the-loop approval with a command or argument preview, a risk level when you give one (without `risk`, no badge rather than a guess), and deny with feedback. Y and N work while focus is in the card; ⌘ or Ctrl + Enter approves, optionally page-wide. `critical` actions need a second, confirming press.

A call with a `command` shows it as a command line, with its `cwd` above it, and every other argument as JSON below it: `{ command: 'npm test', env: { NODE_OPTIONS: '…' } }` shows the `env` it runs with, not only `npm test`. Any other input shows as JSON.

Each pending approval sends one decision: a double click, or Y then N, is ignored until `status` changes, the handler's promise settles or the handler throws. `ToolApprovalCard` binds the card to a tool part and to `addToolApprovalResponse`, including the denial reason, and renders nothing for calls that need no approval.

Decisions that a `toolApproval` rule makes on its own (`approval.isAutomatic`) never prompt or take focus, and read "Auto-approved" or "Blocked by policy" rather than as a person's decision. A custom `preview` is your content: with `styles.css`, the kit's rules don't reach into it, and the shortcuts don't act on keys pressed in it, or in a menu it opens in a portal, so its controls keep their keys. With `globalShortcut`, ⌘ or Ctrl + Enter still approves from it, as from anywhere on the page but a text field.

### Once, for the session or always

`decisions` sets the choices: `allow-once`, `allow-session`, `allow-always`, `deny-once` and `deny-always`. The default is Approve and Deny, once. A lasting choice says what it will cover: the tool, and argument patterns the person can edit. For a command, the pattern is its program and subcommand (`npm test*`), or the whole command when it has no subcommand or runs what its arguments say (`rm -rf build`, `node -e "…"`); for other calls, it starts at each argument as it is. "Any arguments" covers every call of the tool. `onDecide` receives `{ decision, reason, input, args }`; when you pass it, `onApprove` and `onDeny` are not called. With a `policy` from `useApprovalPolicy`, `ToolApprovalCard` offers every choice, records the rule a person makes, and answers a call a rule already covers without showing a card. Once answered, that call reads "Allowed by your rule" and shows the rule.

### Edit the arguments, then approve

With `editable` (on `ApprovalCard`) or `onEditInput` (on `ToolApprovalCard`), "Edit arguments" opens the arguments for editing. An object of strings, numbers and booleans gets a form; any other input gets JSON, which must parse before anything can be approved. The edited input goes out with the approval, with the editor open or closed: "Done editing" closes the editor and keeps the edit, and the card then shows the edited arguments, in its own preview in place of a custom `preview`, with the note "Edited: approving runs the new arguments." "Undo edits" drops the edit. While the JSON doesn't parse, "Done editing" leaves the editor open with its error. An edit belongs to the call it was made for: new `input`, or a new `status`, drops it. `ToolApprovalCard` offers editing only with `onEditInput`, the function that applies the edit, so an edit approved on its card reaches the agent. With AI SDK 7, `onEditInput={(id, input) => setMessages((m) => setToolInput(m, id, input))}` puts the edited input in the messages; the server runs the call with it after checking it against the tool's schema and its `toolApproval` again. With `useAgUiAgent`, pass its `editInput`, and the approval's resume payload carries the edit as `editedArgs`, the field AG-UI agents read.

### Approve all

`ToolApprovalBatch` answers every approval a run is waiting on at once, such as parallel calls; with a critical one among them it asks for a second press. It answers with the agent's arguments and doesn't see an edit made on a card, so approve an edited call on its own card. `AgentMessage` shows it above its cards when two or more approvals are waiting.

### Held keys, focus and the page-wide shortcut

A held key decides once: its repeats don't answer again or confirm a `critical` action, and neither does Enter held on a focused button, Approve all included, or the second click of a double click. The first press on a `critical` action stays armed, with no time limit, until the second press, Escape, or focus leaving the card (or Approve all); the Escape that closes the reason field closes only the field. The press is for the arguments it would approve, so new ones from the agent, or an edit made after it, need a new first press. `autoFocus` moves focus to a card that arrives pending, but not out of a text field, so the letters someone is typing in a chat composer don't land on the card as shortcuts. `globalShortcut` approves with ⌘ or Ctrl + Enter from anywhere on the page except a text field, where the keys stay the field's, such as a chat composer's send; the text fields of the card's own argument editor count too. A press it arms on a `critical` card from outside the card ends when focus or a click goes anywhere but the card, so after typing in a composer and sending, the next ⌘/Ctrl+Enter is a first press again. With several pending, as when `AgentMessage`'s `approvalProps` turns it on for parallel calls, the keys answer one card: the one they're pressed in, or else the latest to ask. When a newer card takes them over, a press they armed on a `critical` card from outside it is dropped.
